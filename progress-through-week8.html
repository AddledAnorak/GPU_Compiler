GLSL reaches a visible NIR trace

Intent: establish what Mesa does to a small shader before our GPU compiler becomes involved.

  • Input. Small vertex and fragment GLSL files, including constant colour and arithmetic examples.
  • Method. A GLES2 harness asks Mesa to compile/link the files; NIR_DEBUG=print records the intermediate code.

The GLSL-to-NIR dictionary explains what survives

Intent: connect familiar shader constructs to actual NIR, including the effect of Mesa optimization.

  • Arithmetic. *, +, and / appear as fmul, fadd, and fdiv early; constant expressions can collapse before the backend sees them.
  • Conditional. One colour-choice if becomes a comparison and b32csel in the final softpipe trace.

Basic NIR arithmetic emits MGC instructions

Intent: prove the backend can turn an arithmetic chain into executable FP16 ISA text.

  • Input. A Mesa NIR shader containing fadd, fmul, and fdiv.
  • Translation. MGC emits fadd16, fmul16, fdiv16, materializes constants, and inserts required waits.
  • Proof. The assembler encodes the output; the emulator executes it and checks the resulting value. The supported subset is still small.

Complex control flow and built-ins are traced

Intent: observe NIR forms that simple constant-colour shaders never expose.

  • Runtime loop. A uniform trip count leaves a NIR loop, a comparison, break, and counter arithmetic.
  • Discard. A conditional that rejects a fragment becomes terminate_if, rather than a simple value selection.
  • Transcendentals. sin/cos appear as fsin/fcos; Mesa expands atan into arithmetic and sign handling.

Student BWeeks 5–6Complete in software

Sine, cosine, and atan reach the emulator

Intent: make Mesa's more complex NIR arithmetic run on the MGC instruction subset.

  • sin/cos. Mesa's lower_sincos rewrites each function into fractional-part and arithmetic operations; MGC emits FP16 instructions.
  • atan. Mesa already builds a polynomial and sign handling; MGC implements the needed reciprocal, selection, masks, and register reuse.
  • Checked. Assembly is encoded, round-tripped, executed in the emulator, and compared numerically. The project suite reported 399 passed, 8 skipped.

Student CWeeks 5–6Incomplete

Binary loading and submission are still missing

Target: take compiled shader instructions from Mesa, put them where the FPGA can read them, and start execution.

  • Current code. The compiler keeps assembly text. Shader binding is empty; draw_vbo and flush do not upload or launch a program.
  • Direct hardware blockers. We need the instruction-memory map and size, plus the controls for loading, starting, and stopping a program.
  • Encoding to confirm. The assembler currently assumes sub-operation and immediate encodings. These details must match the FPGA before real instruction words can be trusted.
  • Further rendering gap. Uniform-buffer loads, vertex inputs, and rasterization need a data path beyond this minimal load-and-run checkpoint.
